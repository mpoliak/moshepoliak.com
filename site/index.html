There are many ways to say the same thing.

And the same words can mean different things, depending on how and where they are said. Yet people understand each other remarkably well. I study how: how listeners infer what a speaker meant, and how speakers shape what they say to help them.

Principles of human cognition should hold in every language, so I test them across many, often with undergraduate researchers who run studies in their native languages. I am a PhD student in Brain and Cognitive Sciences at MIT, advised by Ted Gibson.

Current work

How listeners infer what was meant

Listeners combine the signal with expectations about both meaning and structure, from word order in Russian and Hindi to misheard song lyrics.

  • Russian

How speakers make informative words stand out

Informative and corrected words are said longer, louder, and at a higher pitch, in every language tested so far, including languages said not to use prosody this way.

17 languages in experiments · 22 in conversation corpora

How speakers order words for the listener

Speakers save informative words for later in the sentence, when the listener has more context to interpret them.
